iOS Scan App

TrueToForm’s companion scan app is the core software behind all of the company’s other solutions, using its proprietary scanning algorithm to capture highly accurate measurements. Those measurements are then generated into an avatar for use in garment design, manufacturing, and shopping.

context

The scan app was the foundation of our garment design and fit solutions. Without it, there was no scan data, and without scan data, there was no TrueToForm.

As our solutions expanded and our client base grew, it became clear the app had to succeed.

To achieve that success, we needed to make the scan process WAY more intuitive and usable, despite the complex needs of our scanning algorithm.

Improving the avatar creation flow

I started by replacing the original landing page, a single CTA with no context, with a familiar sign-up pattern and a “continue as guest” option. That balanced leadership’s need for accounts with lower friction for new users.

After conducting competitive research and interviews, I moved away from a linear process. Interview participants called it “long and grueling,” and a less linear flow would make later adjustments easier.

The “avatar hub” design broke the steps into digestible pieces and also avoided the extra development complexity of a linear process.

Taking a successful scan

The trickiest part of designing the scan flow was guiding users through unfamiliar 3D interactions while meeting the scan algorithm’s needs: enough space and distance from the device, correct device placement, a full 360° spin, and more.

My goal was to build robust support systems that guide users through the scans in real time, while keeping implementation cheap.

~Getting ready to scan

One long instructional video didn’t prepare people well, as a large percentage of people skip tutorials. Testing and metric data revealed that clothing selection and location choices were the main reasons scans failed.

Swapping the video for a checklist gave garment choice more weight and was easier to digest.

Test participants reported that vertically scrolling through the checklist made the process feel lighter and shorter.

~Device & position setup: First, do your best

Getting users to understand how their space, position, and movements related to their device placement was tricky. 

Testing revealed that users didn’t understand how device placement and body position depend on each other. Instead of moving the device, they would contort themselves to fit its initial placement.

I used a “level-up” system: first, users followed their own instincts to fit themselves into a silhouette, then the next step helped them fine-tune.

~Device & position setup: Fine-tune

Once users have done their best to position their device and themselves within the silhouette, pressing start takes them to the “fine-tuning” step.

I set up real-time verbal and visual guidance based on where the user’s joints sit relative to the tracking boundaries.

Scan capture started automatically once users were in position, so they didn’t have to break their pose.

The full body scan uses different joint targets based on device position and the complexity of additional joint detection.

~Completing a 360 spin

Once the green check appears, users are told to hold their arms up and wait for the scan capture to begin. 

A beep counts down to a single 10-second spin, since turning too fast or too slow would ruin the point cloud capture.

*Note: A full-screen mobile viewport is just a zoomed-in 3:4 frame. Going full screen would have broken the algorithm and narrowed the user’s horizontal view.

~Tutorials & scan support

Users don’t like being dragged through tutorials. Along with short pre-scan videos, I added “refresher” instructions in the moments they’re relevant.

Links to TrueToForm’s Help Center also appear throughout the app.
