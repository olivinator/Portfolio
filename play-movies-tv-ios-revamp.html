Play Movies & TV iOS Redesign

Play Movies & TV is Google’s source for movie and show streaming. The iOS app didn’t drive enough revenue to earn resources beyond general upkeep. But once we discovered how much playback came from its small user base, we were given light resources to revamp it for its core users.

My role

Lead designer for the iOS redesign
Design and delivery strategy, developer and cross-org collaboration

Team

UX researcher, PM, team leads, the Material team, and a data analyst

Outcome

Shipped MVP: redesigned home, detail page, and library, plus a new illustration style

context

Understanding the platform's value

iOS wasn’t a major revenue driver for the Play org, as purchases weren’t available through the App Store. It was still valuable, though: analytics showed that a small share of users accounted for an outsized share of playback.

1%

Of all Play Movies & TV users were on iOS

=

8%

Of ALL playback, from ALL platforms

Users bought content on their Android or other devices, then used their iOS devices to play back what they’d purchased or downloaded.

Looking into platform usage, 53% of iOS playback was on iPad and 43% of playback was offline.

With that knowledge, a redesign project was born. The goal: to improve the experience for our iOS users.

Guiding principles

Don't impede playback.

Give users power over their content.

Limitations

The time and effort allotted to the iOS redesign was minimal, which heavily restricted any large changes… at least for version one.  

To deliver the redesign on time, leadership deemed the discovery experience low priority, and it was removed. That meant we also removed any features or functionality that promoted purchasing.

Removal of content discovery
Limited support, time, and bandwidth

Remember future plans

There were loose plans to reintroduce content discovery later. To reduce future effort, we decided to keep features that would make that transition easier.

There were also efforts to bring ad-supported video on demand (AVOD) to the Android client. I needed a design strategy that could accommodate AVOD as a potential replacement for content discovery on iOS.

Add content discovery back in... eventually

Pushing beyond MVP

There were many more ways to reach our project goals and honor our principles. I came up with a variety of additional directions and features based on a plethora of past user feedback, should more bandwidth become available. 

We were shifting our model from transactional to consumption-based, so I needed to rethink our primary interactions and information hierarchy around consumption needs.

Transactional needs

Detailed content info for purchase evaluation

Personalized purchase suggestions

Optimized content discovery feed

Consumption needs

Downloads & device storage

~Downloads tab

“I want to download my content but need to see if I have enough room on my device”

43% of iOS users watched content offline, so a shortcut to their downloads, plus a way to manage them, would help users get to their content faster.

A downloads tab is also a great solution for offline mode. 

*Although iOS has a large iPad user base (53%), explorations are shown on mobile.

~Library filtering

“It’s hard to navigate through my library to find what I need”

Since local search wouldn’t be available for a while, filters were the next best thing. Play Books already had a filtering component system we could reuse.

~Hide content (archive)

“I don’t want certain content in my library anymore”

Even on Android, users were begging for a way to archive content. iOS could lead the way. 

Archiving would give users control over older or “embarrassing” content. Archived content could be retrieved from the Play Store.

~Detail page restructure

“I want the detail page to cater to my watch state and give me cues on my watch progress”

If we’re focusing on users’ WATCHING needs, the detail page hierarchy should reflect that rather than emphasizing purchase information. Reorganizing it would get users to their content faster. 

This layout is similar to YouTube’s, which is built for fast playback.
